# Business Entities

## Overview

Businesses exist to make a profit. They can operate as **sole trader**, **partnership**, or **limited liability company** (LLC). The legal structure determines:

- Who bears risk (liability)
- Who gets profit (taxation)
- Who needs to see financial statements (reporting obligations)

## Sole Trader

- **Key features**: Single owner, no separate legal identity, unlimited liability, personal tax, no mandatory publication of financial statements.
- **Advantages**: Full control, easy setup, no publication requirement, flexibility.
- **Disadvantages**: Unlimited liability (personal assets at risk), difficulty raising finance, business ceases on owner's death/retirement.

## Partnership

- **Key features**: Two or more owners, no separate legal identity, unlimited liability, personal tax, partnership agreement governs operations, no mandatory publication.
- **Advantages**: Defined structure, shared skills, risk spread, easier finance via new partners.
- **Disadvantages**: Slow decision-making, profit sharing, personal assets at risk, partnership dissolves on partner departure unless agreement allows continuation.

## Limited Liability Company (LLC)

- **Key features**: Separate legal entity, shareholders have limited liability (only lose investment), corporate tax, mandatory publication of financial statements (IFRS), directors may run the company.
- **Advantages**: Limited liability, easier access to finance (issue shares), business continues regardless of ownership changes.
- **Disadvantages**: Higher setup costs, more administration, double taxation (corporate + personal), public financial statements.

## Why LLC Must Follow IFRS

Limited companies raise capital from the public (shareholders, lenders). They need to provide **comparable**, **credible**, and **reliable** financial statements to attract investment. IFRS provides a common framework that enables investors to compare companies across jurisdictions. Sole traders and partnerships typically rely on owner's capital or private loans, so they don't face the same accountability.

## Key Implication

- **Sole trader/partnership**: Financial statements are usually for tax authorities or banks; not required to publish.
- **LLC**: Must prepare a full suite of statements (including SOCF and SOCE) and file them publicly.

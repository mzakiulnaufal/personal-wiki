# Financial Reporting

## Definition

Financial reporting is the **recording, analysing, and summarising** of financial data to present the financial performance and position of a business.

- **Recording**: Capturing transactions (e.g., sales, purchases) accurately and promptly.
- **Analysing**: Categorising transactions and **interpreting** them to identify trends, main expenses, cash sources, etc. (This includes both sorting and interpretation.)
- **Summarising**: Compiling detailed records into annual financial statements that give an overview for users.

## The Four Main Financial Statements

### 1. Statement of Financial Position (SOFP / Balance Sheet)

- **Snapshot** at a specific date.
- Shows **assets** (resources controlled), **liabilities** (obligations), and **equity** (owners' interest).
- Helps assess financial strength, liquidity, and solvency.

### 2. Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income (SPLOCI)

- **Flow** over a period.
- Shows **profit or loss** (income minus expenses) and **other comprehensive income** (unrealized gains/losses, e.g., revaluation surplus).
- Indicates whether the business is profitable.

### 3. Statement of Cash Flows (SOCF)

- **Flow** over a period.
- Shows **cash inflows and outflows** categorised into operating, investing, and financing activities.
- Explains why cash balance changed between SOFP dates.
- **Mandatory only for limited companies.**

### 4. Statement of Changes in Equity (SOCE)

- **Flow** over a period.
- Reconciles equity balance at start and end: profit/loss, share issuance, dividends, revaluation gains.
- **Mandatory only for limited companies.**

## Relationships Between Statements

- **SPLOCI → SOCE → SOFP**: Profit from SPLOCI flows into equity via SOCE, which updates the equity figure in SOFP.
- **SOFP (start) → SOCF → SOFP (end)**: SOCF explains the cash movement between the opening and closing cash balances on SOFP.
- **SOFP ↔ SOCE**: Equity changes are detailed in SOCE.
- **Key insight**: A company can be profitable but have negative cash flow (e.g., if profits are tied up in receivables or inventory). SOCF reveals this.

## Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)

OCI consists of items not recognised in profit or loss because they are **unrealized**. Common example: revaluation surplus on property. OCI is reported in the statement of comprehensive income (part of SPLOCI). Only companies are required to present OCI.

## Non-Financial Reports

Companies often include non-financial information in their annual reports, such as:
- Strategic report, corporate governance, sustainability disclosures (IFRS S1 and S2), environmental impact, etc.
- These complement financial statements to give a fuller picture.
